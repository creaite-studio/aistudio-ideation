Last night at 10pm, the rice paddies turned on. 🐸

10,000 frogs. All at once.

Today marks micro-season #19 of Japan's ancient 72-season calendar: 蛙始鳴 — "Frogs begin to sing."

This is what happens when farmers flood the rice fields. Frogs that have been waiting underground all winter appear overnight, filling the countryside with sound that farmers have used as a planting signal for thousands of years.

The Japanese word for frog (kaeru 蛙) sounds identical to the word for "return home" (kaeru 帰る). So frog figurines are kept in wallets to make money return, and near doorways to bring travelers home safely.

Matsuo Bashō wrote the most famous haiku in Japanese history about a frog jumping into a pond. He was just paying attention.

That's all the 72 micro-seasons ask: pay attention to the texture of right now.
